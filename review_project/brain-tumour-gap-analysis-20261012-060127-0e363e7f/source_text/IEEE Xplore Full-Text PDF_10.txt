    medical image segmentation is effectively handling multiscale
    change targets, which refer to the significant variations in
    size, shape, and morphology exhibited by segmentation targets
    (such as organs or lesions) across different images or even
    within the same image. Traditional convolution operations,
    employing fixed-size kernels and static receptive fields, strug-
    gle to adapt to this inherent variability. To overcome this
    limitation, our CNN branch utilizes the dynamically adap-
    tive DDConv in each stage. Unlike traditional fixed kernels,
    DDConv dynamically learns both deformation offsets and
    weight coefficients based on the input features. The learned
    offsets allow the receptive field’s shape and sampling locations
    to adapt to the specific geometry of organs or lesions, while
    the learned weight coefficients adjust the feature emphasis
    dynamically. This dual adaptivity makes DDConv particularly
    adept at capturing features across diverse scales and com-
    plex shapes, enabling the network to better understand local
    semantic features, perceive subtle changes, and accurately
    extract these multiscale change targets. Similarly, the cross-
    dimensional fusion Transformer branch also consists of seven
    main stages. By employing (S)W-ACAM attention in each
    stage, as illustrated in Fig. 2(b), the segmentation network
    gains enhanced understanding of the global dependencies
    within medical images. This allows it to effectively capture
    positional information between different organs and improve
    the separability between foreground and background.
