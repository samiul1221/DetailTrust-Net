MBUNeXt: Multibranch Encoder Aggregation
Network Based on Layer-Fusion Strategy for
Multimodal Brain Tumor Segmentation
Qinghao Liu , Yuehao Zhu, Min Liu , Senior Member, IEEE, Zhao Yao , Yaonan Wang ,
and Erik Meijering , Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Multimodal brain tumor segmentation (BraTS),
integrated with surgical robots and navigation systems, enables
accurate surgical interventions while maximizing the preservation
of surrounding healthy brain tissue. However, multimodal brain
scans suffer from large interclass differences in brain tumor
subregions and information redundancy, leading to inadequate
fusion of multimodal information and significantly affecting the
accuracy of BraTS. To address the above problems, we propose
a multibranch encoder aggregation (MEA) network based on a
layer-fusion strategy called multibranch UNeXt (MBUNeXt). The
network comprises three well-designed modules: the multimodal
feature attention (MFA) module, the MEA module, and the
large-kernel convolution skip (LCS)-connection module. These
modules work together to achieve precise segmentation of brain
tumors. Specifically, the MFA module preserves the intermodality
similarity structure through attention mechanisms and Gaussian
modulation functions, thereby filtering redundant information.
Then, the MEA module exploits the correlations among multiple
modalities to effectively integrate multimodal hybrid feature
representation and optimize multimodal information fusion.
In addition, the LCS module constructs multiple groups of
depthwise separable convolutions with large kernel, which can
guide the network to attend to features at different scales,
thereby addressing the issue of significant interclass differences
in brain tumor subregions. The experimental results on the
large-scale public datasets, BraTS2019 and BraTS2021, which
consist of approximately 5000 3-D brain scans, demonstrate that
our proposed method has achieved SOTA performance, with
average Dice scores of 85.84% and 91.11%, respectively. It also
performs well on the BraTS-Africa2024 dataset with low imaging
quality, confirming its robustness. The code is available at https://
github.com/liuqinghao2018/MBUNeXt
Index Terms—Brain tumor segmentation (BraTS), layer-fusion
strategy, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), multimodal fusion.

I. INTRODUCTION
BRAIN tumors can cause severe neurological defects and
even pose a life-threatening risk. In modern biomedi-
cal image processing, segmentation [1], [2], [3] techniques
are widely utilized for the diagnosis, staging, and follow-
up of brain tumors. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) [4]
is a widely adopted imaging modality in the automated
diagnosis of brain tumor diseases due to its ability to pro-
vide high-contrast images of soft tissues. With different
parameter acquisitions, a range of MRI sequences with dif-
ferent modalities can be obtained [5]. For the diagnosis of
brain tumor diseases [6], MRI modalities commonly utilized
include T1-weighted (T1), post-contrast T1-weighted (T1Gd),
T2-weighted (T2), and T2 fluid-attenuated inversion recovery
(FLAIR). Among them, T1 can reveal various tomographic
anatomical structures; T1Gd can visualize areas with enhanced
blood supply after the injection of a contrast agent, providing
further insight into the tumor’s internal characteristics; T2 is
related to the water content in tissues and organs, and can
precisely locate the area of the lesion; FLAIR can present the
surrounding conditions of the tumor site, showing the edema
area. Different imaging modalities can present complementary
information regarding the lesion area and surrounding tissues.
Therefore, the use of multimodal medical images can make
up for the shortcomings of single-modality images, effectively
helping doctors to accurately identify the surgical area and
lesion structure. The integration of medical image analysis
techniques with the application of surgical robot systems in
modern medicine enables more precise and refined surgical
interventions, maximizing the preservation of surrounding
normal brain tissue and enhancing the safety and efficacy of
the surgery [7], [8], [9].
However, the vast amount of medical data associated with
multimodal MRI scans has greatly increased the workload of
disease diagnosis. Doctors need to invest additional time in
manually assessing an extensive volume of medical images,
which can result in diagnostic errors. Hence, there is an urgent
need to develop a multimodal MRI segmentation method
that can effectively leverage the complementary information
present in multimodal images and extract regions of interest
(lesion tissues and organs) from these images.
In recent years, significant progress has been made in
the field of deep learning, with various deep neural net-
works demonstrating versatility and efficiency in medical
image segmentation. These networks have outperformed tra-
ditional algorithms, yielding notable results. U-Net [10],
a symmetric encoder–decoder network structure, has been

particularly influential in medical image segmentation. Its

a leading network in the field. Khan et al. [11] integrated
deep saliency maps and an improved dragonfly algorithm,
enhancing the accuracy of brain tumor detection and classifi-
cation through feature enhancement, selection, and extreme
learning machine (ELM) classification. Rajinikanth et al.
[12] employed VGG-UNet-assisted segmentation and sup-
port vector machine (SVM) classification to enhance the
detection and classification accuracy of brain tumors in
MRI slices through feature extraction, selection, and fusion.
Maqsood et al. [13] used a deep neural network and multiclass
SVM (M-SVM), enhancing the precision of brain tumor
detection and classification through image contrast enhance-
ment, customized convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
segmentation, transfer learning-based feature extraction, and
entropy-based feature selection. Badjie and Ülker [14], based
on the deep transfer learning architecture of AlexNet, improve
the accuracy of brain tumor detection and classification in
MR images through feature enhancement, selection, and an
optimized network structure. In addition, TransBTS [15]
employs 3-D CNNs to extract spatial features and uses a
Transformer for global feature modeling, ultimately achieving
image segmentation through a convolutional decoder. This
method differs from traditional approaches that rely only on
CNNs, as it combines the strengths of local feature extraction
with global information. It demonstrates excellent performance
in tasks such as multimodal brain tumor segmentation (BraTS)
and classification.
Taking into account the characteristics of multimodal MRI
scans, and the advantages and disadvantages of existing meth-
ods, we design a multibranch encoder aggregation (MEA)
network based on a layer-fusion strategy to achieve accurate
segmentation of brain tumor. Specifically, three well-designed
modules are introduced into the U-shaped network to solve
practical problems in multimodal BraTS, such as inadequate
multimodal information fusion, information redundancy, and
large interclass differences of brain tumor subregions.
To effectively integrate the multimodal image informa-
tion obtained from the multibranch encoder, we propose a
multimodal feature attention (MFA) module, which utilizes
an attention mechanism to guide the fusion process and
assist the multibranch encoder to extract multimodal infor-
mation. Importantly, by embedding Gaussian functions in
the MFA, the module cannot only emphasize mutual fea-
tures of multimodal images, but also filter out subrelated
information.
To aggregate the unique features of different modal images,
an MEA module is introduced, which connects the multibranch
encoder and decoder. The module converts the multimodal
features extracted from the multibranch encoder into tokens
and inputs them into the Transformer for global multimodal
information modeling.
To guide the network to focus on the target area, effectively
identify lesions of varying sizes, and address the issue of size
variation, a large-kernel convolution skip (LCS)-connection
module is designed. In the LCS module, multiple sets of depth-
wise separable convolutions with large kernel are constructed
to achieve a larger receptive field and capture multiscale
context information [16], [17].
Integrating these modules into the network architecture,
we propose a new architecture called multibranch UNeXt
(MBUNeXt).
The experimental results of MBUNeXt on several highly
challenging multimodal BraTS datasets, particularly for
BraTS2019 and BraTS2021, provide compelling evidence of
the effectiveness of our model, which surpasses other existing
BraTS methods and demonstrates its outstanding performance
in this field. To further validate its robustness, we have
conducted experiments on the BraTS-Africa2024 dataset as
well, achieving good segmentation results and proving the
method’s strong robustness.
In summary, the contributions of this work are as follows.
1) A U-shaped network named MBUNeXt is proposed
for multimodal BraTS. The network is designed to
emphasize and aggregate the potential shared features of
multimodal images and filter out redundant information,
while achieving a larger receptive field at a global
scale, enabling the network to capture multiscale feature
information, emphasizing target areas, and accurately
locating lesions.
2) The MFA module is proposed to emphasize potential
shared features and filter out redundant information,
which integrates multimodal feature maps in the channel
dimension and constructs Gaussian modulation func-
tions to adaptively adjust the activation response of
the segmentation target. The proposed MEA module
fully aggregates multimodal information and learn com-
plementary features between multiple modalities by
modeling nonlinear relationships within and between
modalities. The LCS module addresses the issue of sig-
nificant interclass scale differences between brain tumor
subregions. It achieves this by employing multiple sets
of depthwise separable convolutions with a large kernel,
allowing for the extraction of lesion feature information
at different scales.
3) Extensive experiments were conducted on two large-
scale multimodal MRI BraTS tasks (BraTS2019 and
BraTS2021), which included approximately 5000 3-D
brain scans. To further validate the robustness of the
method, experiments were also carried out on the BraTS-
Africa2024 dataset. The results demonstrate that the
proposed method achieved superior outcomes compared
to the current SOTA approaches, even with fewer param-
eters, proving its robustness and broad applicability.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Medical Image Segmentation Methods
Utilizing the strong feature extraction capability of CNNs,
the fully convolutional network (FCN) [18] employs CNNs
for end-to-end semantic segmentation. U-Net [10] has been
widely used for a variety of medical image segmentation
tasks and has inspired a great deal of research to adapt to
specific segmentation needs or improve performance. Subse-
quently, many networks were designed that use this U-shaped
architecture as the backbone, such as Res-UNet [19], UNet++
[20], and KiU-Net [21], which further advanced the progress
of medical image segmentation. nnU-Net [22] demonstrated
the effectiveness of a generalized U-Net architecture with
minimal modifications in achieving remarkable results across

handling multiscale features.
Recent Transformer-based models excel in computer vision
by modeling long-range dependencies. In medical image
segmentation, U-shaped architectures integrated with Trans-
formers [24] achieve SOTA performance. For example,
UNETR [25] designed a U-shaped network that combines a
Transformer and CNN. It utilizes ViT [26] as an encoding
module to extract contextual information and employs CNN
to capture local information, leading to the final segmentation
results. Swin UNETR [27], on the other hand, uses Swin
Transformer [28] as an encoder to compute self-attention
in a shifted window and also enables further processing of
the extracted multiscale features by patch merging. Swin
UNETR has demonstrated exceptional performance in a wide
range of medical image segmentation tasks. DeepCRC-SL [1]
constructs a 1-D colorectal coordinate system to transform
labels into coordinate maps. It enhances the segmentation con-
tinuity through auxiliary regression tasks, differentiates tissues
using self-attention layers, and adopts a coordinate-driven self-
learning strategy to integrate unlabeled data for performance
improvement. It performs remarkably in colorectal cancer
and colorectum segmentation. HResFormer [3] combines 2-D
and 3-D Transformers, utilizes the hybrid local–global fusion
module (HLGM) to integrate information, and adopts the
residual learning of the hybrid model. It outperforms existing
methods in multiple medical image segmentation benchmark
tests. However, these methods [15], [29], [30], [31] incorporate
self-attention mechanisms into the encoding process, which
demands larger datasets to effectively learn input sequence
relationships for improved output prediction.
B. Multimodal Fusion Methods
The purpose of multimodal fusion is to utilize the infor-
mation from various modalities of medical images to assist
in data representation, enhance the discriminative capability
of networks, and thereby improve the accuracy of multimodal
medical image segmentation. Based on deep learning, fusion
strategies [32] can be categorized into three types: input-level
fusion, layer-level fusion, and decision-level fusion.
In the input-level fusion strategy, these methods fuse mul-
timodal images channel by channel into multichannel inputs,
learn the feature representation after fusion, and then train
the segmentation network. Three-dimensional ShiftBTS [33]
extends ShiftViT to 3-D multimodal medical images, introduc-
ing a 3-D shift operation, enhanced feature extractor (EFE),
and cascaded attention module. It achieves superior perfor-
mance in complex BraTS while maintaining computational
efficiency. Zhou et al. [34] proposed a multitask segmentation
network on the BraTS dataset, which fused multimodal MRI
scans channel by channel, learned the feature representation
after fusion in the input space, decomposed the BraTS task into
three different but related subtasks, and gradually trained them.
This method achieved the best performance on the BraTS2015
and BraTS2017 datasets. Most of these methods focus on
subsequent complex segmentation network architecture design,
without considering the relationships between different modal-
ities, and without analyzing how to fuse different feature
information to improve segmentation performance.
In the layer-level fusion strategy, these methods utilize two
or more modal images as a single input to train segmentation
networks. Subsequently, the learned feature representations
from these inputs are fused across the layers of the network,
and the fused results are then fed back to the decision layer to
obtain the final segmentation output. Dolz et al. [35] proposed
a 3-D fully CNN based on DenseNets, which extended dense
connections to accommodate multimodal segmentation. Each
imaging modality has a pathway containing layers with dense
connections, enabling the network to learn more complex
feature representations. It demonstrated SOTA performance in
multimodal brain tissue segmentation challenges. M2
FTrans
[36] framework integrates a modality-masked fusion Trans-
former with learnable tokens and masked self-attention
mechanisms, optimizing multimodal feature fusion through
spatial-weighted attention and channel-wise fusion Transform-
ers. It achieves significant performance improvements over
existing methods on the BraTS dataset, particularly excelling
in critical scenarios where dominant modalities are absent.
AAHN [37] proposes an asymmetric dual-stream encoder with
Transformer-CNN branches, employs the Transformer-CNN
feature alignment and fusion (T-CFAF) module and cross-
modality heterogeneous graph fusion (CMHGF) module for
adaptive feature fusion, and achieves superior performance
on six medical image segmentation datasets. PDMINet [38]
introduces a prototype-driven framework with multimodal
information interaction, prototype-guided fusion, and multi-
expert integration to address feature aliasing in BraTS. It
achieves superior performance on BraTS datasets by enhanc-
ing subregion-specific features and suppressing irrelevant
information. However, the large number of dense connections
within each pathway introduces a significant number of param-
eters, making the network difficult to train. Moreover, while
multimodal medical images provide information at different
levels, they also introduce redundant data and substantial
noise, which may degrade network performance when using
overly simplistic dense connections.
Decision-level fusion focuses more on fusion issues because
each modality is treated as an input to a separate network.
These networks [39], [40] can learn the complex and com-
plementary feature information of each modality, and finally,
fusion is performed at the output layer of the network to
obtain the segmentation results. Nie et al. [41] proposed a
novel multi-FCNs network architecture dedicated to infant
brain tissue segmentation, employing a multimodal network
training and high-level feature fusion strategy to achieve more
accurate segmentation. In general, compared to input-level
fusion and layer-level fusion, if the fusion method is effective
enough, decision-level fusion can achieve better segmentation
performance. However, decision-level fusion requires multiple
network architectures to individually extract information from
each modality, leading to an exponential parameter increase.
This demand for more memory and computational resources
limits its practical application. Hence, adopting an appropriate

III. METHOD
A. Overview
MBUNeXt is a novel U-shaped network that incorporates
multiple encoding branches and one decoding branch. The
overall architecture of the MBUNeXt network is illustrated
in Fig. 1. Specifically, MBUNeXt consists of four main
components: the multibranch encoder, the MFA module, the
MEA module, and the LCS module. This section provides an
overview of the general structure of MBUNeXt and further
elaborates on significant implementation details of the model,
such as how the MFA module effectively emphasizes potential
shared features, how the MEA module fully aggregates multi-
modal complementary information, and how the LCS module
guides the network to focus on feature information at different
scales.

tinct MRI modalities separately. The multibranch encoder
extracts the unique information of each modality step by step
to obtain deep feature maps of multiple modalities. In most
methods, multiple feature maps are fused by concatenation,
addition, and multiplication.

difficult to fully aggregate multimodal information. Given the

Specifically, the MEA module captures the multimodal feature
map MA4 from the multibranch encoder.

E. Skip-Connection With Large-Kernel Convolution
In the context of medical image segmentation, the primary
objective of neural networks is to accurately identify and
segment the lesion area. However, in brain tumors, there are
often several different tissue subregions with large differences
in size and shape. Accurately identifying the location and
boundaries of morphologically diverse lesions poses a chal-
lenge for networks utilizing a single conventional 3 × 3 ×
3 convolution. To overcome these difficulties, we design the

F. Decoder
The decoder obtains the multimodal feature map ME that
has been meticulously modified by the MEA module, gradu-
ally restores it to the original 3-D image size (H × W × D),
and produces segmentation results. Specifically, at each stage,
the encoder features from the LCS module are concatenated
with those corresponding to the decoder. The concatenated
features are processed by 3 × 3 × 3 convolutions, and then
upsampled by transposed convolutions until they are restored
to the original input resolution. Finally, the features at the
original image resolution are processed through a sigmoid
activation function and a 1 × 1 × 1 convolutional layer for
generating a multimodal medical image segmentation map.

IV. EXPERIMENT
The proposed MBUNeXt model, along with other SOTA
segmentation methods, underwent training and testing on
two challenging multimodal brain tumor datasets, namely,
the BraTS2019 and BraTS2021 datasets. In addition, the
BraTS-Africa2024 dataset was incorporated into the validation
process, allowing for the assessment of the MBUNeXt model’s
robustness across datasets with unique imaging characteris-
tics using identical experimental settings. The segmentation
methods were evaluated based on common performance
metrics. Furthermore, the effectiveness of the modules pro-
posed within MBUNeXt was demonstrated through ablation
studies.
A. Dataset and Implementation
1) BraTS2019 Dataset: This dataset [49], [50], [51], pro-
vided by the BraTS 2019 Challenge, focuses on glioma
segmentation and includes multimodal MRI data from 19
institutions. The training set consists of 335 cases, each
with 3-D MRI scans in four modalities: T1, T1Gd, T2, and
T2-FLAIR. Images are resized to 155 × 240 × 240 voxels.
Labels include the nonenhancing tumor core (NCR), peritu-
moral edema (ED), and enhancing tumor (ET), forming three
evaluation regions: the whole tumor (WT, including NCR, ED,
and ET), the tumor core (TC, including NCR and ET), and
ET, as shown in Fig. 5. We adopted Swin UNETR [27]’s data
partitioning method, splitting the training set into an 80%:20%
ratio and training networks using fivefold cross-validation. Our
method was compared with various SOTA approaches for a
comprehensive evaluation.
2) BraTS2021 Dataset: This dataset [50], [51], [52] was
provided by the BraTS 2021 challenge and included additional
routine clinically acquired 3-D multimodal MRI scans. It
consists of a total of 5004 3-D brain scans. Apart from
the number of volumes, all other information regarding the
BraTS2019 and BraTS2021 datasets remained identical. In
the BraTS2021 challenge, the training set consisted of 1251
cases. Compared to the training set of BraTS2019, BraTS2021
contained approximately four times more data. This significant
increase in data volume was a crucial factor in selecting these
two datasets for our experiments. It allows us to demonstrate
that our network could still achieve high performance despite
the substantial difference in the amount of available data.
3) BraTS-Africa2024 Dataset: This dataset [53] is derived
from the MICCAI-CAMERA-Lacrina Fund BraTS-Africa
2024 Challenge, with a training set consisting of 60 cases.
Similar to the BraTS2019 dataset, each case includes MRI
scans of four modalities and shares the same segmentation
targets. However, unlike BraTS2019, this dataset is character-
ized by the widespread use of low-quality MRI technology,
resulting in poorer image quality and resolution, which poses
additional challenges for the segmentation task.
4) Experiment Details: The MBUNeXt model was imple-
mented based on PyTorch and trained with an NVIDIA Quadro
RTX A6000 GPU. During the training phase, a batch size of 2
was set, and the model was trained for 300 epochs. The Dice
loss function was employed to update the network weights.
The AdamW optimizer was utilized with an initial learning
rate of 0.0001, and it underwent linear warm-up and cosine
annealing to gradually adjust the learning rate. In addition,
various general data augmentation techniques were applied.
Specifically, during the training process, when trained with
the BraTS2019, BraTS2021, and BraTS-Africa2024 datasets,
the input images were randomly cropped to 128 × 128 ×
128 voxels. For the 3-D images, random mirroring flips were
performed along the axial, coronal, and sagittal planes with a
probability of 0.5. In addition, random intensity shift within
the range of (−0.1, 0.1) and random intensity scale within
the range of (0.9, 1.1) were applied to the images. Prior to
training, all images were normalized to have zero mean and
unit standard deviation. The Dice loss function was employed
to update the network weights, which is defined as

V. CONCLUSION
This article presents a novel MEA network based on a
layer-fusion strategy for multimodal BraTS. The network
incorporates three well-designed modules to enhance its seg-
mentation performance. The proposed MFA module fuses
the multimodal features of multibranch encoders at different
stages, emphasizes the potential shared information in dif-
ferent modalities, and filters out redundant information. And
the MEA module aggregates multibranch encoders, model
the nonlinear relationship among different modalities, and
effectively integrating multimodal information. In addition,
the LCS module guides the network in attending to feature
information at various scales, enabling accurate identification
of lesions of different sizes and addressing the issue of size
variability. Comprehensive experiments were conducted on
the BraTS2019, BraTS2021, and BraTS-Africa2024 datasets
to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The
experimental results show that MBUNeXt achieves SOTA
performance in multimodal BraTS with a smaller number
of parameters, by effectively extracting features from each
modality and comprehensively fusing multimodal information,
while also demonstrating good robustness.
