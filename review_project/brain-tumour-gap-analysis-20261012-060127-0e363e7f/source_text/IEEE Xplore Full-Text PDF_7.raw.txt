SwinPA-Net: Swin Transformer-Based Multiscale
Feature Pyramid Aggregation Network for
Medical Image Segmentation
Hao Du, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Jiazheng Wang , Graduate Student Member, IEEE,
Min Liu , Member, IEEE, Yaonan Wang , and Erik Meijering , Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—The precise segmentation of medical images is
one of the key challenges in pathology research and clinical
practice. However, many medical image segmentation tasks have
problems such as large differences between different types of
lesions and similar shapes as well as colors between lesions and
surrounding tissues, which seriously affects the improvement of
segmentation accuracy. In this article, a novel method called
Swin Pyramid Aggregation network (SwinPA-Net) is proposed
by combining two designed modules with Swin Transformer
to learn more powerful and robust features. The two modules,
named dense multiplicative connection (DMC) module and local
pyramid attention (LPA) module, are proposed to aggregate the
multiscale context information of medical images. The DMC
module cascades the multiscale semantic feature information
through dense multiplicative feature fusion, which minimizes
the interference of shallow background noise to improve the
feature expression and solves the problem of excessive variation
in lesion size and type. Moreover, the LPA module guides the
network to focus on the region of interest by merging the
global attention and the local attention, which helps to solve
similar problems. The proposed network is evaluated on two
public benchmark datasets for polyp segmentation task and skin
lesion segmentation task as well as a clinical private dataset for
laparoscopic image segmentation task. Compared with existing
state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods, the SwinPA-Net achieves the
most advanced performance and can outperform the second-best
method on the mean Dice score by 1.68%, 0.8%, and 1.2% on
the three tasks, respectively.
Index Terms—Dense multiplicative connection (DMC) mod-
ule, local pyramid attention (LPA) module, medical image
segmentation.

I. INTRODUCTION
DEEP-LEARNING-BASED image segmentation plays a
critical role in medical image analysis and has achieved
impressiveimpressive performance in several medical tasks [1],
[2], [3], such as the segmentation of organs and tissues in var-
ious types of microscopic images [4], [5], polyp segmentation
in colonoscopy images [6], [7], and skin lesion segmentation in
dermoscopy images [8]. Accurate and efficient medical image
segmentation can be employed not only to identify the features
of the lesion areas but also provide a reliable basis to aid
clinical diagnosis and condition research [9], [10], [11], [12]
and has potential application value in surgical navigation and
disease progression monitoring.
In recent years, convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
such as ResNet [13] have shown powerful feature extrac-
tion capabilities. Subsequently, the fully convolutional net-
work (FCN) [14] creatively replaced the fully connected
layer in CNN with the convolutional layer to preserve the
spatial information of the image for semantic segmentation
tasks [15]. Inspired by FCN, a typical U-shaped network,
named U-Net [16], is proposed to overcome problems on med-
ical image segmentation. By using the encoder–decoder struc-
ture and skip connections, the network can learn the multiscale
features properly and has performed well in many medical
image segmentation tasks, quickly becoming a baseline for
comparing the state-of-the-art (SOTA) medical image segmen-
tation algorithms. With the popularity of U-Net, some inno-
vative models based on the U-shaped architecture have been
proposed and achieved good results, such as UNet++ [17],
SegNet [18], DenseUNet [19], KiU-Net [20], and CAB U-Net
[21]. However, the convolutional-based deep learning networks
have inherent defects, such as the difficulty of accurately
defining the boundary features and the lack of global con-
text information, which limits its further improvement in the
performance of biomedical image segmentation tasks.
To address the above issues, several novel approaches have
been developed in recent years, such as the Mumford–Shah
model, contrastive learning, and pyramid structure. Methods
based on the Mumford–Shah model [22], [23] extract accurate
boundary information of the original image through piece-
wise smoothing functions and optimization strategies, while
methods based on contrastive learning [24], [25] regulate
the distance between semantic information by exploring the

complexity of these methods is often very high, and it is
difficult to accurately segment different types of lesions with
large differences. The pyramid structure [26], [27], [28], [29]
obtains multiscale contextual information by utilizing different
convolution rates or multiresolution input feature maps. For
example, Deeplab [26] adopts multiple convolution rates with
different receptive fields to improve the multiscale information
extraction ability of the model. SSPP [27] utilizes different
stride pooling operations to generate multiresolution feature
maps. However, it is difficult for these methods to effectively
identify small local features, and the perceptual field of these
methods is not well adjusted to obtain the best features for
lesions of different sizes at each stage.
Inspired by the huge achievements of transformers in
natural language processing (NLP) tasks [30], several deep
learning networks with transformer backbone have been uti-
lized in the computer vision (CV) domain [31]. The vision
transformer (ViT) [32] is the first transformer architecture
designed for image classification, which encodes the input
image as two-dimensional flattened patches with the location
embeddings, producing convincing results on ImageNet by
pretraining on a large dataset. Following the technical route
of ViT, data-efficient image transformer (DeiT) [33] can
learn the local features through the distillation token, and it
can achieve good performance in smaller datasets because
of the data-efficient training strategy. Although the above
transformer-based architecture can reach SOTA in some image
recognition tasks, these networks generally have high demands
on computer hardware and face the lack of generalization
abilities. To solve these problems, Swin Transformer [34] pro-
poses a self-attention mechanism based on shifted windows,
which improves the generalization of the model and greatly
reduces the computational complexity through cross-window
connections. It has achieved SOTA performance in different
kinds of tasks, such as image classification, object detection,
and semantic segmentation.
In this article, motivated by the success of Swin Trans-
former and other novel methods mentioned earlier, we adapted
this work to the area of medical image segmentation and
introduced two designed modules into the U-shaped network
with Swin Transformer as backbone to solve the problems
that still exist in medical image segmentation. For instance,
there is a large gap between different types of lesions in
various medical image datasets, with lesions having similar
shapes and colors to the surrounding tissues and low contrast,
as shown in Fig. 1. Specifically, the two modules, named dense
multiplicative connection (DMC) module and local pyramid
attention (LPA) module, are proposed to aggregate the multi-
scale context information of different kinds of medical images.
The DMC module cascades the multiscale semantic feature
information through multiplicative feature fusion, guiding the
network to focus on feature information of different scales,
which can reduce the interference of shallow feature noise
and improve the expression of features. Furthermore, the DMC
module can help the network to better identify lesion edges
in different sizes and solve the problem of size changes. The
LPA module uses a pyramid module to aggregate attention

With the above descriptions, we name the method as Swin
Pyramid Aggregation network (SwinPA-Net). The proposed
SwinPA-Net is tested on three challenging medical image
segmentation tasks: polyp segmentation in colonoscopy
images, skin lesion segmentation in dermoscopy images, and
the segmentation of loose connective tissue in laparoscopic
images. The first and second tasks are based on public
benchmark datasets, while the third task is based on a clinical
private dataset.
The contribution of this work can be summarized in the
following three areas.
1) A U-shaped network named SwinPA-Net is proposed,
which introduces two designed modules into the network
with Swin Transformer as backbone. During encod-
ing, the proposed model employs Swin Transformer to
extract features, enabling the network to have global
and dynamic receptive fields through self-attention and
shifted window mechanisms. Also, the DMC and LPA
module are proposed to enhance the feature extraction
capability of the SwinPA-Net. The DMC module adopts
a DMC method, which can reduce the interference
of noise on the shallow features, so that the shallow
information containing more boundary details can be
obtained. The LPA module aggregates attention cues
in different scales as well as guide the network to
enhance semantic features and emphasize the target area,
resulting in accurately locating the lesion. Moreover, the
SwinPA-Net utilizes CNN as the decoder to restore and
integrate the local and global feature maps to obtain
more powerful and robust features.
2) By evaluating the proposed method on three challenging
medical image segmentation tasks (polyp segmentation
in colonoscopy images, skin lesion segmentation in
dermoscopy images, and the segmentation of loose

II. METHOD
A. Overview
In this section, the overall structure of the proposed
SwinPA-Net is introduced in detail, as shown in Fig. 2. It is
a U-shaped structure based on the decoder and encoder. First,
we introduce the Swin Transformer structure used in the model
and then introduce other important implementation details of
the model, such as how the DMC module effectively integrates
contextual multiscale feature information and how the LPA
module combines local and global features.
B. Swin Transformer Encoder
A standard transformer structure first splits the input image
equally into a series of nonoverlapping patches, and then,
the pixels of these patches are linearly flattened into several
1-D vectors. Afterward, these vectors are passed through the
multihead self-attention (MSA) module to calculate the self-
attention and would be finally processed using Gaussian error
linear unit (GELU) nonlinearity and multilayer perception
(MLP) layers, while each module is preceded by a LayerNorm
(LN) layer followed by a residual connection.
Due to the division of the image into small patches, the
standard transformer needs to calculate relationships between
these patches, where implies quadratically growing computa-
tional cost. Therefore, Swin Transformer employs window-
based MSA (W-MSA) and shifted window-based MSA
(SW-MSA), which changes the regular window partitioning
method and uses a more efficient shifted window partitioning
strategy, as shown in Fig. 3(a). Not only does it maintain
efficient computation of nonoverlapping windows, but it also
establishes connections between different windows. Two suc-
cessive Swin Transformer blocks use W-MSA and SW-MSA
modules, respectively, to establish the connection between
different windows, as shown in Fig. 3(b). ẑl

C. DMC Module
In the medical image segmentation tasks, we hope that the
network can focus on the target area, but due to the different
sizes of the targets, especially when some lesion areas or
anatomical landmarks are relatively small, it is difficult to
locate the target area accurately. The main reason is that
the feature maps generated by the encoder would become
increasingly coarse due to continuous downsampling. For a
small target area, the boundary information in the deep feature
maps would be turned into a few pixels or even lost altogether,
which has a great impact on the accuracy of the medical image
segmentation. A simple method is to input a large-size image
to obtain a larger feature map, preserving the information of
the lesion area as much as possible, but this would incur a huge
computational cost. Another method is to fuse shallow features
and deep features, where the shallow feature map contains a lot
of detailed boundary information. However, since the shallow
feature map simultaneously retains some noise and most
feature fusion methods would mix some irrelevant information

each other, thereby filtering out the noise and obtaining more
accurate prediction results.
D. LPA Module

mentation network would only focus on the target area of the
lesion. However, due to the large difference between the types
of lesions in various medical images and the small difference
between the color of the lesion and the surrounding tissues,
it is difficult for the network to accurately identify the location
of the lesion. At the same time, the network needs to learn the
complex structure of lesions with different sizes. In order to

network to enhance the distinction of semantic features and

The attention mechanism consists of two attention blocks:
the spatial attention (SA) and channel attention (CA) block.
Not all regions in the image are equally important in
contributing to the task, and thus, the SA module [39] is

find the attention maps Pi (X) of different layers and would
finally be merged to obtain the final attention map. This can
effectively enhance the discriminative ability of each feature
in feature learning and improve the ability of the network to
recognize the target area. At the same time, we embed the
proposed LPA structure into the four outputs of the DMC
module.
E. Decoder
The decoder part combines the convolution blocks, upsam-
pling, and skip connections to recover the resolution of the
feature map stage by stage, as shown in Fig. 2. Specifically,
at each stage, the intermediate feature map is first fused with
the feature information cascaded from the same stage through
the DMC and LPA modules output, adjusted by 3 × 3 convo-
lution and upsampled using bilinear interpolation, and finally,
the output of that stage is obtained using 3 × 3 convolution to
improve the upsampling results. In particular, in the final stage
of the encoder path, the feature map, which is processed from
the previous stage, is recovered to the same size as the input
image by the upsampling operation and 1 × 1 convolution
adjustment of the number of channels.

III. EXPERIMENTS
The proposed SwinPA-Net and other SOTA segmentation
methods were trained and tested under the same conditions
on three challenging medical image datasets called the Polyp
dataset, the ISIC 2018 dataset, and the LIVis dataset. Common
metrics were used to evaluate the performance of the proposed
segmentation method. Also, an ablation study was conducted
to demonstrate the effectiveness of different modules in the
SwinPA-Net.
A. Datasets and Implementation
1) Polyp Dataset: The polyp dataset consists of five
public datasets named Kvasir [43], CVC-ClinicDB [44],
CVC-ColonDB [45], EndoScene [46], and ETIS [47]. These
datasets contain 2248 images of different sizes and are from
different medical institutions. To ensure fairness, we adopted
the division method in PraNet [6], where the images from
Kvasir and CVC-ClinicDB datasets are randomly split into
90% for training (1450 images in total) and 10% for testing
(162 images in total). To confirm the generalization ability
of the proposed method, the CVC-ColonDB, EndoScene, and
ETIS datasets (636 images in total) which did not appear in
the training set were also used as the testing set.
2) ISIC 2018 Dataset: The ISIC 2018 dataset comes from
the Lesion Boundary Segmentation task in ISIC-2018 chal-
lenge [48], which contains 2594 images from different types of
skin lesions collected by several leading international clinical
centers. These skin lesion images of different resolutions have
corresponding manual labels as the reference standards for
segmentation evaluation, and the labels have been continu-
ously confirmed and fixed by experts. Fivefold cross-validation
dataset was performed in the experiment, which split the
dataset into 2075 images for training and 519 images for
testing.
3) LIVis Dataset: LIVis dataset is a clinical private dataset
collected from six live TME surgeries conducted on six
patients using the surgical robot, as shown in Fig. 6. The

E. Ablation Study
In this section, to prove the feature extraction capability
and the effectiveness of different modules for the proposed
SwinPA-Net (Model 4), an ablation study is conducted on the
Kvasir dataset, as shown in Table V. The mIoU and mDice
scores are employed to evaluate the segmentation performance
in the ablation study.
Model 1 and 2 are built to compare the performance of
traditional CNN and transformer structure to extract features.
Model 1 changes the encoder of the U-Net structure to
ResNet-152 as the backbone of feature extraction, and Model 2
uses Swin Transformer to extract features while maintaining
the same structure as Model 1 in the decoder. By comparing
with Model 1, we can see that the accuracy of segmentation
has improved in Model 2, which proves the effectiveness of
Swin Transformer as encoder to extract features.
Moreover, Models 3 and 4 are established to evaluate the
usefulness of the DMC module and the LPA module, where
the DMC module is added in Model 3 based on Model 2, and
Model 4 adds the LPA module to Model 3. By comparing
Models 2–4, we see that the introduction of the DMC module
and the LPA module can considerably improve the mIoU and
mDice scores. This is because the DMC module can fuse the
features of different scales, while the LPA module can extract
higher level information from local and global features, so that
the model can better focus on useful features while suppressing
less important features.
In addition, Models 5 and 6 are constructed to compare the
effects of different feature fusion methods on the segmentation
results, where Model 5 utilizes the additive fusion and Model
6 applies the concatenate fusion to replace the DMC module.
By comparing Models 4–6, it can be seen the DMC module
(Model 4) can obtain better feature expression through mutual
constraints between multiscale features, which shows that the
DMC module is more suitable for our method.

respect to the optimal number of layers and different parameter

Models 7 and 8 use one- and three-layer LPA modules, respec-

than a one-layer global attention, and when the number of

IV. CONCLUSION
This article presents SwinPA-Net, which is a novel Swin
Transformer-based multiscale feature pyramid aggregation net-
work for medical image segmentation. The core idea of
SwinPA-Net is to aggregate the context and boundary informa-
tion of medical images through the Swin Transformer encoder
and the designed DMC and LPA modules, while filtering out
the noise and irrelevant information in the shallow features.
The designed modules can effectively alleviate the problem
that the lesions are difficult to locate accurately due to the large
difference between the types of lesions in various medical
images and the small gap between the color of the lesion and
the surrounding tissues.
